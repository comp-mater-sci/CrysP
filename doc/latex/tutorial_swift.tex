\hypertarget{tutorial_swift_autotoc_md42}{}\doxysection{Introduction}\label{tutorial_swift_autotoc_md42}
This tutorial gives a step-\/by-\/step procedure for the identification of hardening parameters of a micro-\/scale $\tau(\gamma)$ hardening law, such as Swift law and single-\/ or double-\/stage Voce laws, from macro-\/scale experimental stress-\/strain data. Note that all hardening laws in VEF are micro-\/scale laws and are thus defined on the slip-\/system level. As example, we use macro-\/scale stress-\/strain data of a uniaxial tensile test along RD of a polycrystalline DX51D steel sheet sample to identify the corresponding micro-\/scale Swift law.

\begin{DoxyNote}{Note}
A similar procedure may also be followed for Voce law or any other micro-\/scale hardening law that prescribes $\tau(\gamma)$. The methodology can also be readily adapted for identification against another (single) monotonic test.
\end{DoxyNote}
The micro-\/scale Swift law postulates the Critical Resolved Shear Stress of a grain $\tau$ as a function of a single internal state variable, being the accumulated slip $ \gamma $ of that grain, as\+: \[ \tau(\gamma) = \tau_0 \left(\frac{\gamma}{\gamma_0} + 1 \right)^n \] This heuristic hardening law has 3 parameters\+: $\tau_0$, $\gamma_0$ and $n$.

In what follows 2 approaches to obtain $\tau(\gamma)$ from the macro-\/scale experimental stress-\/strain data $\sigma(\epsilon)$ are demonstrated\+:


\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item \mbox{[}Taking texture evolution into account\mbox{]} (\mbox{\hyperlink{tutorial_swift_swift_texture_evol}{Swift identification with texture evolution}})~\newline
 Deformation-\/induced changes in texture that affect the stress-\/level of the macroscopic flow stress $\sigma(\epsilon)$ are considered.  
\item \mbox{[}Neglecting texture evolution\mbox{]} (\mbox{\hyperlink{tutorial_swift_swift_texture_const}{Swift identification with constant texture}})~\newline
 Texture is assumed constant during the macro-\/scale test.  
\end{DoxyItemize}

The macro-\/scale stress-\/strain curve (also called identification test) is given in the file {\ttfamily examples\textbackslash{}tutorial\+\_\+swift\textbackslash{}DX51\+D\+\_\+uni0\+\_\+exp.\+txt}. \begin{DoxyAttention}{Attention}
A set of micro-\/scale hardening parameters is identified under assumption of specific multi-\/scale model configuration, such as the \mbox{[}model type\mbox{]} (model\+\_\+type) (e.\+g. Alamel or Taylor) and the \mbox{[}definition of slip systems\mbox{]} (slip\+\_\+system\+\_\+family). Even if the hardening law is the same, the parameters are generally different for different multi-\/scale model assumptions. So in case several variants of multi-\/scale model are being studied, it is important that the identification procedure is repeated for every model variant.
\end{DoxyAttention}

\hypertarget{tutorial_swift_autotoc_md43}{}\doxysubsection{Basics of the identification strategy}\label{tutorial_swift_autotoc_md43}
The identification strategy consists of three steps\+: 
\begin{DoxyEnumerate}
\item {\bfseries{Non-\/hardening VEF simulation of the identification test}}~\newline
 A non-\/hardening VEF simulation is conducted first. It is hereby assumed that the Critical Resolved Shear Stress equals unity, independent of the degree of deformation. Quantities associated to this non-\/hardening reference material are tagged by superscript-\/star symbol ($\ast$). So for instance, we have the relation $ \tau^* \equiv 1 $, and the tensile stress and accumulated slip of the non-\/hardening material are written as $ \sigma^*_{xx} $ and $ \gamma^* $, respectively. The goal of the non-\/hardening simulation is to obtain the relationship $\sigma^*_{xx}(\gamma^*)$.  
\item {\bfseries{Swift parameter identification through curve fitting}}~\newline
 Parameter identification of the $\tau(\gamma)$ hardening law is based on the {\itshape approximate} relationship between the experimental target tensile stress $\sigma_{xx}$ on one hand, and quantities of the non-\/hardening material on the other hand\+: \[ \sigma_{xx} \approx \sigma^*_{xx}(\gamma^*) \tau(\gamma^*) \] In general, this relation is only approximate since the right-\/hand side neglects local interaction effects between grain orientations (reflected by the factor $ \sigma^*_{xx}$) and strain hardening (given by the factor $\tau(\gamma^*)$). It has nevertheless proven to be a usefull relation in practice. In case of the of Swift law, expansion of the $\tau(\gamma)$ relation leads to \[ \sigma_{xx} \approx \sigma^*_{xx}(\gamma^*) \tau_0 \left(\frac{\gamma^*}{\gamma_0} + 1 \right)^n \] Simultaneous identification of the three Swift parameters $\tau_0$, $\gamma_0$ and $n$ can be accomplished by, for instance, least-\/square fitting, i.\+e. the minimization of following function of the parameters\+:\label{tutorial_swift_eq_leastsquarefit}%
\Hypertarget{tutorial_swift_eq_leastsquarefit}%
 \[ f\left(\tau_0,\gamma_0,n\right)=\sum_{i} \left[ \sigma^{exp}_{xx,i} - \sigma^*_{xx,i} \tau_0 \left(\frac{\gamma^*_{i}}{\gamma_0} + 1 \right)^n \right]^2 \] Note that the experimental datapoints $\sigma^{exp}_{xx,i}$ and the non-\/hardening simulation output data ( $\sigma^*_{xx,i}=\sigma^*_{xx}(\gamma^*_{i})$ and $\gamma^*_{i}$) need to be known for the same set of deformation levels $i$.  
\item {\bfseries{Verification of parameter identification}}~\newline
 As the identification relationship is approximate, it is advised to check that a satisfactory simulation accuracy is obtained for the identification test using the hardening parameters identified in previous step.  
\end{DoxyEnumerate}

\item The experimental datapoints $\sigma^{exp}_{xx,i}$ for strain levels $i$ are located in {\ttfamily examples\textbackslash{}tutorial\+\_\+swift\textbackslash{}DX51\+D\+\_\+uni0\+\_\+exp.\+txt}.

\item Wait a minute -\/ how about the datapoints of homogenized accumulated slip $ \gamma^* $? On first sight, it does not appear to be listed in the {\ttfamily uds}-\/output file... But in fact we {\itshape do} know it, because for the non-\/hardening material with unity CRSS, $ \gamma^* $ is numerically identical to the accumulated work $ W^* $, which we can find back in column 7. It can be derived from the rate of work of a particular grain due to slip activity on slip systems $s$, which is given by $ \dot{w} = \sum_{s}\tau_{s} \dot\gamma_{s} $. For the non-\/hardening material with $ \tau^*_{s}\equiv 1 $, it follows that rate of work in the grain equals the total slip rate of the grain. The identity $ W^* = \gamma^* $ is then found after time integration and volume-\/averaging (i.\+e. homogenization). 
\end{DoxyItemize}

Least-\/square fitting results in the following parameter set\+: $\tau_0=43.38MPa$, $\gamma_0=1.35\space10^{-2}$ and $n=0.243$.

The identification strategy consists of three steps\+: 
\begin{DoxyEnumerate}
\item {\bfseries{Non-\/hardening VEF simulation of the identification test}}~\newline
 Similar to the \mbox{[}identification procedure with texture evolution\mbox{]} (\mbox{\hyperlink{tutorial_swift_swift_texture_evol}{Swift identification with texture evolution}}) a non-\/hardening VEF simulation is conducted first. However, this time only for a single small increment to determine the initial yield stress. The goal of the non-\/hardening simulation is to obtain the yield stress for the same load direction as the macro-\/scale test assuming $\tau^*=1$ . 


\item {\bfseries{Conversion of macro-\/ to micro-\/scale parameters}}~\newline
 The yield stress obtained from the non-\/hardening simulation is then used to convert the macro-\/scale hardening parameters into the corresponding micro-\/scale counterparts. Here it is assumed that the macro-\/scale hardening law is already known in the following form\+: \[ \sigma_{xx} = \sigma_{xx,0} \left(\frac{\epsilon_{xx}}{\epsilon_{xx,0}} + 1 \right)^n \]

In the present example $\sigma_{xx,0}=112.9$ MPa, $\epsilon_{xx,0}=0.0054$, $n=0.2461$.




\item {\bfseries{Verification of parameter identification}}~\newline
 Lastly, the micro-\/scale law is used to simulate the identification test and to verify the accuracy of the calibration.  
\end{DoxyEnumerate}
